# Views and actions

Read this when producing a turnaround, multiple views, action poses, or an animation reference.

## Identity anchor

Use the supplied reference or an established design as the canonical character. Keep species, silhouette, proportions, face, palette, clothing, accessories, and distinctive markings stable. Change only the requested view, pose, or expression. For a new character, establish a clear base design first; continue directly into requested views or actions unless the user asked to approve stages.

Across a set, keep character scale, ground baseline, lighting, and rendering style consistent. Do not invent hidden back details unrelated to the established design.

For each new view or pose, tell the image tool which supplied image establishes identity and what is allowed to change. Keep that prompt specific to the requested view or action rather than repeating a long generic constraint list. Where supported, attach the identity anchor again for subsequent images; a prior generated variant alone may carry forward drift.

## Turnarounds

- A three-view sheet means front, **true side**, and back unless the user specifies otherwise. A true side is a profile, not another three-quarter view.
- Optional views include three-quarter front and three-quarter back. Label views only when requested or when labels are part of the deliverable.
- Use a neutral pose and aligned proportions. Check that outfit seams, accessories, tail, wings, and armor connect plausibly between views.
- Inspect each view independently for anatomy and identity drift; a plausible sheet layout does not prove the views match.
- For more than the standard three views, establish the requested front, side, and back directions before filling intermediate angles. Define left and right in viewer/screen coordinates when direction matters, and check asymmetrical markings or accessories instead of assuming a flipped view is equivalent.

## Action poses

- Choose a readable key pose with clear weight, ground contact, and limb count. Keep the whole character visible unless framing was requested.
- Walking: opposing arm and leg swing with a clear planted foot. For a cycle, contact, down, passing, and up are useful keys; keep direction, scale, and baseline fixed.
- Running: stronger lean and leg separation, with compression and extension. For a cycle, reach/contact, compression, passing, and airborne/extension are useful keys.
- Gestures such as waving or raising a hand, paw, or wing should change the relevant limb and supporting posture while preserving the body design.
- Avoid motion blur in poses intended as animation references. Add weapons or props only when requested or already part of the design.
- For a cycle, compare frames together at intended display size. Check the planted body or foot anchor, apparent scale, action progression, and the last-to-first transition; revise the failing pose and its neighbors when that transition breaks.

## View vocabulary

- Front: faces camera directly.
- Three-quarter front or back: approximately 30–45 degrees from the corresponding straight view.
- True side: approximately 90-degree profile.
- Back: faces directly away.

Match the user's requested direction exactly; do not substitute a more convenient angle.
