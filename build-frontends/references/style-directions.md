# Frontend Style Directions

Use this reference when a new interface or redesign lacks a settled visual direction. These are design grammars, not templates. Choose from product context, then change the palette, typography, composition, and signature detail to make the result specific.

## How to choose and adapt a direction

Select a direction using four inputs:

1. **Task character:** read, compare, decide, create, monitor, transact, explore, or play.
2. **Audience expectation:** institutional trust, technical precision, warmth, cultural authority, premium exclusivity, or expressive energy.
3. **Content structure:** narrative, catalog, dense data, workflow, media, community, or single-purpose conversion.
4. **Brand constraints:** existing assets, color, type, history, competitors, and explicitly rejected styles.

For each direction, adapt at least three axes:

- Typography or type treatment.
- Layout rhythm and information density.
- Palette and contrast.
- Component geometry.
- Imagery or icon language.
- Motion behavior.
- One product-specific signature element.

Never choose a style only because its sample palette looks attractive. The same direction should look different for a museum, a finance tool, and a restaurant.

## Swiss editorial

**Thesis:** express authority through grid, proportion, typography, and clarity rather than decoration.

**Suitable for:** architecture, design studios, museums, public institutions, research, technical publications, and portfolios.

**Starting palette:** paper `#F5F5F2`, ink `#111111`, muted `#6B6B66`, rule `#C9C9C3`, signal red `#E0362D`. Change the signal color to match the subject.

**Typography:** neutral grotesk or humanist sans; large contrast between display and utility text; disciplined baseline; occasional mono for metadata. Use weight and scale before color.

**Layout:** asymmetrical grid, strong alignment lines, generous margins, numbered or indexed structures only when content has real order. Allow full-width rules and small metadata columns.

**Components:** open sections, lists, indexes, editorial tables, sharp or minimally rounded controls. Cards are rare and purposeful.

**Motion:** precise short transitions, restrained reveals aligned to the grid.

**Signature ideas:** navigational index, changing margin annotation, modular poster-like hero, or typographic data marker derived from the subject.

**Avoid:** turning it into a newspaper caricature, decorative `01/02/03` labels without meaning, or using Helvetica-like type as the only design decision.

## Contemporary editorial

**Thesis:** make content feel curated and authored through art direction, expressive type, image sequencing, and varied pacing.

**Suitable for:** magazines, cultural organizations, fashion, food, travel, interviews, studios, and narrative brand sites.

**Starting palette:** warm page `#F3F0E9`, ink `#201C19`, muted `#756D65`, burgundy `#7D2738`, moss `#566147`. Choose one accent, not both.

**Typography:** characterful serif or display face with a quiet sans for navigation and captions. Use deliberate line breaks, pull quotes, captions, and varied scale.

**Layout:** strong image-to-text rhythm, occasional overlap, wide and narrow measures, offset captions, full-bleed moments, and breathing room between stories.

**Components:** article leads, media frames, pull quotes, story lists, author or source metadata, and minimal navigation chrome.

**Motion:** image reveal, page transition, or one scroll-linked editorial moment; keep reading stable.

**Signature ideas:** a subject-specific typographic masthead, annotated image sequence, or evolving chapter marker.

**Avoid:** warm cream plus serif as an automatic combination, magazine decoration without real content, unreadable display text, or animation that interrupts reading.

## Quiet product minimalism

**Thesis:** reduce cognitive load and make the workflow feel inevitable through clear hierarchy, consistent spacing, and excellent state design.

**Suitable for:** productivity software, account areas, forms, collaboration, developer consoles, internal tools, and B2B products.

**Starting palette:** canvas `#F7F8FA`, surface `#FFFFFF`, text `#172033`, muted `#697386`, border `#DDE2EA`, accent `#2563EB`. Replace blue when brand context permits.

**Typography:** highly readable sans, compact control typography, clear numeric and code treatment. Use modest scale changes and strong weight hierarchy.

**Layout:** stable app shell, restrained container widths, consistent alignment, purposeful whitespace, and density matched to task frequency.

**Components:** toolbars, forms, lists, tables, drawers, dialogs, and command surfaces with complete loading, empty, error, and selected states.

**Motion:** immediate state feedback, subtle expansion and route continuity, little ambient motion.

**Signature ideas:** unusually clear object model, excellent keyboard command layer, product-specific timeline, or calm information reveal.

**Avoid:** generic SaaS hero layouts, floating cards everywhere, excessive muted gray, blue as the only interactive cue, or minimalism that hides functionality.

## Data-dense operations

**Thesis:** prioritize scanning, comparison, control, and anomaly recognition while keeping density legible.

**Suitable for:** analytics, finance, logistics, observability, admin systems, trading, scheduling, inventory, and command centers.

**Starting palette:** canvas `#0F1317`, surface `#171C21`, raised `#20262C`, text `#E7EBEF`, muted `#98A2AD`, grid `#303842`, cyan `#4CC9D8`, warning `#F0A43C`, critical `#EB5A5A`. A light theme may be more appropriate for long daily use.

**Typography:** compact sans with tabular numerals; mono only for identifiers, logs, and code. Distinguish labels, values, deltas, and units.

**Layout:** persistent spatial model, aligned columns, dense rows, adjustable panels, resizable regions, frozen context, and clear focus/selection.

**Components:** tables, timelines, charts, inspectors, filters, saved views, alerts, status rails, and batch actions. Prefer direct labels over mystery icons.

**Motion:** minimal; use motion for streaming updates, causality, and panel transitions without disturbing scanning.

**Signature ideas:** domain-specific status rail, time-axis anatomy, linked brushing, layered alert severity, or spatial overview/detail relationship.

**Avoid:** turning tables into card grids, huge KPI tiles, rainbow charts, color-only status, tiny hit targets, or decorative dark-mode glow.

## Industrial technical

**Thesis:** communicate durability, precision, and directness using strong structure, functional markings, and restrained high-visibility accents.

**Suitable for:** infrastructure, hardware, robotics, manufacturing, cybersecurity, developer tools, terminals, and engineering products.

**Starting palette:** graphite `#111416`, steel `#202529`, text `#E7E9E8`, muted `#929A9E`, rule `#3A4247`, safety orange `#FF5A1F`, instrument green `#7FD18B`.

**Typography:** sturdy grotesk plus mono for measurements, commands, and identifiers. Use uppercase sparingly for control labels and status categories.

**Layout:** rigid grid, exposed system structure, labeled zones, measurement-like spacing, and clear boundaries between control and output.

**Components:** status strips, command bars, schematics, logs, diagnostic panels, toggle groups, and explicit system feedback.

**Motion:** mechanical and short; progress, connection, and state transitions should feel deterministic.

**Signature ideas:** topology map, machine-status motif, live signal trace, or context-aware command surface.

**Avoid:** fake terminal text, meaningless hazard stripes, excessive monospace, low contrast, or science-fiction decoration unrelated to the product.

## Neo-brutalist utility

**Thesis:** make hierarchy unmistakable through raw contrast, strong borders, direct language, and deliberately visible construction.

**Suitable for:** creative tools, experimental portfolios, independent media, events, youth products, music, and opinionated brands.

**Starting palette:** off-white `#F7F4EA`, black `#111111`, electric yellow `#F4E409`, cobalt `#2457FF`, coral `#FF5C5C`. Limit the final interface to one or two accents.

**Typography:** bold grotesk or condensed display paired with a simple text face. Use dramatic scale but keep controls readable.

**Layout:** hard edges, visible grid, strong rules, offset blocks, intentional imbalance, and large type as structure.

**Components:** thick-bordered controls, direct status messages, poster-like panels, and tactile pressed states. Use shadows only when they reinforce physical layering.

**Motion:** snap, slide, or purposeful overshoot; keep durations short and reduced-motion behavior complete.

**Signature ideas:** draggable poster layer, modular marquee tied to real content, or bold mode switch that changes composition.

**Avoid:** random colors, every element having a thick border, inaccessible contrast combinations, cartoonish shadows, or chaos without information hierarchy.

## Humanist service design

**Thesis:** create confidence and calm through plain language, generous readability, humane rhythm, and supportive recovery states.

**Suitable for:** healthcare, education, civic services, nonprofit work, onboarding, support, and personal finance guidance.

**Starting palette:** canvas `#F8F7F3`, surface `#FFFFFF`, text `#24312D`, muted `#66736E`, green `#2F745E`, clay `#C36D4A`, soft blue `#5C7FA3`.

**Typography:** warm humanist sans or highly readable serif/sans pairing; comfortable body size and line height; sentence case throughout.

**Layout:** clear steps, readable measures, stable navigation, supportive whitespace, and progressive disclosure for complex processes.

**Components:** guided forms, checklists, summaries, next-step panels, appointment or task flows, and errors that preserve input and explain recovery.

**Motion:** gentle continuity and confirmation; avoid urgency unless the content truly requires it.

**Signature ideas:** personalized progress map, reassuring evidence panel, or context-sensitive explanation tied to the user's step.

**Avoid:** childish illustrations for serious situations, vague friendliness, hiding difficult information, low-density layouts that make processes feel longer, or green used as the only success cue.

## Playful modular

**Thesis:** encourage exploration through clear modular structure, expressive color, friendly geometry, and responsive feedback.

**Suitable for:** learning, community, creator tools, consumer utilities, family products, hobbies, and playful productivity.

**Starting palette:** canvas `#FFF9ED`, ink `#24202B`, blue `#3973F6`, coral `#FF715B`, lime `#B7E34A`, lavender `#9C7AF2`. Use a dominant accent and reserve others for meaningful categories.

**Typography:** rounded or characterful sans paired with a neutral body face. Keep playful display decisions away from dense control text.

**Layout:** modular zones, friendly scale shifts, visible progress, occasional controlled asymmetry, and generous touch interaction.

**Components:** collections, progress paths, creation blocks, reactions, lightweight rewards, and direct manipulation.

**Motion:** spring-like feedback and staged success moments with strict limits and reduced-motion alternatives.

**Signature ideas:** manipulable object system, progress landscape, responsive mascot behavior, or content blocks that visibly assemble.

**Avoid:** rainbow decoration, every object becoming a pill, gamification unrelated to user goals, childish copy for adult audiences, or motion on every hover.

## Luxury restraint

**Thesis:** communicate value through proportion, material imagery, typographic refinement, and controlled scarcity rather than ornamental excess.

**Suitable for:** fashion, hospitality, beauty, jewelry, architecture, premium food, galleries, and high-end services.

**Starting palette:** black `#0D0D0D`, ivory `#F4F1EA`, stone `#B7B0A7`, oxblood `#5A1F2B`, muted gold `#A58A58`. Use metallic tones sparingly and avoid fake gradients that imitate foil.

**Typography:** refined display serif or distinctive high-contrast face paired with restrained sans. Use careful tracking, line breaks, and generous but not empty spacing.

**Layout:** strong photography, slow visual rhythm, asymmetric crops, minimal chrome, and deliberate moments of full bleed.

**Components:** quiet navigation, product or venue storytelling, reservations or purchase paths, detailed media, and discreet controls.

**Motion:** slow but responsive fades, image transitions, and spatial continuity; never delay essential action.

**Signature ideas:** material close-up sequence, editorial product choreography, spatial reveal, or typographic monogram used with restraint.

**Avoid:** gold everywhere, thin low-contrast text, excessive letter spacing, huge empty screens, ornamental cursors, or sacrificing conversion and accessibility for mood.

## Natural material and craft

**Thesis:** connect digital experience to origin, material, place, and process through texture, honest imagery, and grounded typography.

**Suitable for:** food, sustainability, outdoors, agriculture, crafts, home goods, wellness, and local hospitality.

**Starting palette:** oat `#EDE4D3`, earth `#6F4E3D`, forest `#274C3A`, clay `#B85C3C`, charcoal `#262724`, sky `#8FB7C9`.

**Typography:** practical serif or soft humanist sans; use hand-made lettering only as a limited authentic accent.

**Layout:** tactile photography, ingredient/material stories, process sequences, organic but aligned cropping, and spacious catalog or editorial rhythm.

**Components:** provenance details, seasonal availability, product collections, maps, process timelines, and care instructions.

**Motion:** subtle parallax, texture transitions, or process reveals; keep scrolling and reading stable.

**Signature ideas:** origin map, material cross-section, seasonal color shift, or process illustration based on real production.

**Avoid:** generic leaf icons, greenwashing claims, fake paper texture on everything, rustic script overuse, or low-contrast beige-on-beige interfaces.

## Retro-futurist interface

**Thesis:** reinterpret a specific technological era through modern usability rather than reproducing a costume.

**Suitable for:** games, music, events, experimental tools, creative portfolios, media launches, and speculative products.

**Starting palette:** midnight `#0A0C18`, screen blue `#45A3FF`, phosphor `#B7F34A`, magenta `#F34BC8`, text `#E6ECFF`, grid `#26304D`. Use one primary luminous accent.

**Typography:** geometric display or era-informed bitmap/mono accent paired with a modern readable interface face.

**Layout:** layered instrument panels, scan-like divisions, cinematic titles, grids, and controlled depth. Keep interaction zones obvious.

**Components:** transport controls, sequencers, timelines, media browsers, maps, synth-like parameter groups, or game HUD elements.

**Motion:** scan, pulse, waveform, and spatial transitions tied to real state. Respect reduced motion and avoid constant background animation.

**Signature ideas:** responsive waveform, navigable star/map system, instrument dial language, or era-specific boot transition that does not block repeat visits.

**Avoid:** random neon, unreadable monospace, fake code, CRT effects over all content, excessive glow, or nostalgia that obscures usability.

## Immersive media

**Thesis:** let high-quality media carry narrative and emotion while navigation and copy remain precise and unobtrusive.

**Suitable for:** film, travel, entertainment, art, architecture, sports, product launches, and interactive storytelling.

**Starting palette:** derive from the media. Use a stable neutral UI layer—near-black `#101010` or white `#FAFAF8`—plus one sampled accent.

**Typography:** confident display scale with quiet interface labels. Keep essential copy readable independent of image brightness.

**Layout:** full-bleed media, controlled overlays, chapter sequencing, spatial transitions, and alternating immersive and readable sections.

**Components:** media rails, chapters, maps, galleries, timelines, playback, captions, and contextual facts.

**Motion:** cinematic transitions, media-aware scroll, and progressive reveal; provide controls and reduced-motion alternatives.

**Signature ideas:** scene-to-map transition, layered before/after comparison, interactive timeline, or media sequence responding to product state.

**Avoid:** using a video as a substitute for information architecture, text over busy imagery without contrast control, autoplay audio, heavy assets blocking first content, or scroll hijacking.

## Mixing directions safely

Choose one base grammar and at most one secondary influence:

```text
Base direction: controls layout, hierarchy, density, and component geometry.
Secondary influence: affects one limited axis such as type, color, imagery, or motion.
Product signature: introduces one subject-specific element not borrowed from either style.
```

Examples:

- Swiss structure + natural material imagery for a sustainable architecture studio.
- Quiet product minimalism + industrial status language for infrastructure software.
- Contemporary editorial + playful modular color for a youth culture magazine.
- Data-dense operations + humanist service copy for a healthcare scheduling console.
- Luxury restraint + immersive media for a destination hotel.

Do not combine several display fonts, competing grid systems, multiple radius languages, and unrelated motion styles. Coherence matters more than the number of inspirations.
